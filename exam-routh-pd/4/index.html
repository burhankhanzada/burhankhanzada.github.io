Maths note · solving an inequality, and the one move that flips it

Half the answers this course asks for are ranges rather than numbers — the gains that keep a loop stable, the parameters for which a solution exists at all — and a range comes out of an inequality. Every move you know from equations still works on one: add to both sides, subtract from both sides, multiply, divide. There is exactly one exception, and it is where the marks go.

Multiply or divide by a negative number and the sign turns round. Check it on numbers you can see: 3>13 > 13>1 is true, and multiplying both sides by −1-1−1 gives −3-3−3 and −1-1−1 — and −3-3−3 is less than −1-1−1. Adding and subtracting never do this; only the two moves that can change a sign do.

Taking a root of both sides needs both sides non-negative. That is why l2>a2{\htmlClass{mc2}{l}}^2 > {\htmlClass{mc2}{a}}^2l2>a2 may be shortened to l>a{\htmlClass{mc2}{l}} > {\htmlClass{mc2}{a}}l>a here: l{\htmlClass{mc2}{l}}l and a{\htmlClass{mc2}{a}}a are lengths, so both are positive and the absolute values never arise. Where a quantity could be negative, the honest form is ∣l∣>∣a∣|{\htmlClass{mc2}{l}}| > |{\htmlClass{mc2}{a}}|∣l∣>∣a∣ and it stays that way.

Combining is an overlap, not a list. Two conditions on the same parameter are both true at once, so the answer is the piece of the line where they agree:

If two conditions never overlap, that is also an answer — it says no value of the parameter works, and for a controller gain it says this controller cannot stabilise this plant.
