Shaky on the maths? Open: linearising = replacing a curve by its tangent

Maths note · linearising = replacing a curve by its tangent

Curves are hard, straight lines are easy. So near the point you actually operate at, you throw the curve away and keep the straight line that touches it there.

f(x)≈f(x0)+f ′(x0)⏟⋅(x−x0){\htmlClass{mc2}{f}}({\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}) \approx {\htmlClass{mc2}{f}}({\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}}) + \underbrace{{\htmlClass{mc2}{f}}\,'({\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}})}\cdot({\htmlClass{mc3}{x}} - {\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}})f(x)≈f(x0​)+f′(x0​)​⋅(x−x0​)

In words, and this is the whole idea:

x0{\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}}x0​the operating point — where the machine normally sits f(x0){\htmlClass{mc2}{f}}({\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}})f(x0​)the value there, a plain number f ′(x0){\htmlClass{mc2}{f}}\,'({\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}})f′(x0​)the slope there, also a plain number — the sensitivity x−x0{\htmlClass{mc3}{x}} - {\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}}x−x0​your small deviation, usually renamed Δx\Delta {\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}Δx

So a nasty curve becomes Δf=(a number)⋅Δx\Delta {\htmlClass{mc2}{f}} = (\text{a number}) \cdot \Delta {\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}Δf=(a number)⋅Δx — proportional again, and everything in control engineering works on it.

The price. It is only true near x0{\htmlClass{mc3}{x}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st0}{0}}x0​. Drive the system far from that point and the model quietly stops describing reality.
