Right

Ask the two questions the diagram answers: how many inputs, and how many outputs? One in, two out. Every option except the last is a single transfer function, and a single transfer function is one input to one output — so four of the five are the wrong shape before any of them is the wrong formula.

H1(s)=G1(s)U(s),H2(s)=G2(s)U(s){\htmlClass{mc5}{H}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st1}{1}}({\htmlClass{mc1}{s}}) = {\htmlClass{mc4}{G}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st1}{1}}({\htmlClass{mc1}{s}}){\htmlClass{mc8}{U}}({\htmlClass{mc1}{s}}), \qquad {\htmlClass{mc5}{H}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st2}{2}}({\htmlClass{mc1}{s}}) = {\htmlClass{mc4}{G}}_{\htmlClass{subtag st2}{2}}({\htmlClass{mc1}{s}}){\htmlClass{mc8}{U}}({\htmlClass{mc1}{s}})H1​(s)=G1​(s)U(s),H2​(s)=G2​(s)U(s)

Stacked, that is a column of two transfer functions multiplying one input. It is the simplest multi-output system there is, and it is worth recognising because the notation is the door into state space, where one input driving several outputs is the normal case rather than the exception.

Why "series" is the tempting wrong answer. Two tanks in a picture usually are in series — one drains into the next, which is the coupled two-tank system, and it is second order. The word the question leans on is uncoupled: same inflow, no path between them. Read that word and four options disappear.
Hint 1 of 2
Count the inputs and count the outputs before looking at the options.
Hint 2 of 2
Series means the output of one is the input of the next. Parallel means their outputs are added. Is either true here?
